# Sandbox: changing a chart someone else made

A part of [Sandbox](vfs://guides/sandbox.md).

## Changing a chart someone else made

A saved query belongs to its workspace, and other people read it: a dashboard's tile, an app's chart, a For you card, a chart in a chat. When the person asks you to change such a chart, such as "this by quarter" or "every platform, not only the top one", never write the query. Try the change in their draft of it, the same way the pencil on the chart does in Kater. Nothing anyone else reads changes until the person keeps it.

A person who can read a chart can always try a change to it in their draft. Never refuse because they can read the workspace and not change it: the draft is in their own brainstorm folder, and it reads the workspace by reference.

1. **Open the draft.** `kater query sandbox <workspace> --request-file -` with `{"document": {"path": "<the query's file>"}}`: the command sends the workspace that `<workspace>` names. The draft copies the question, never the filters: the workspace's filters hold in it, at the reader's choices for this draft, which Kater keeps apart from the file (`dashboard.inputs.read` and `dashboard.inputs.save` with `consumer: {"kind": "draft", "path": "<the draft>"}`). It answers `path`: `users/<their email>/brainstorm/draft_<query name>.sql`, the one they have, as they left it, or a new one with the query's SQL as written and `from:`. `kater query draft` with the same request reads it, or what a new one would hold, and writes nothing; its `save` says what Save may do (below). Never copy the workspace yourself: Save as a duplicate copies it, in one commit, and the copy says where it came from. Never write a field's definition into the draft: `from:` places it in the workspace, so it reads the workspace's fields, filters and queries by reference, and a fix to a definition reaches the draft.
2. **Change it with picks.** `kater scratch statement.read --request-file -` with the scratch `namespace`, the draft's `path` and `statement: 0` reads its picks; `kater scratch statement.write` with the same and the new `picks` answers the statement's new `sql` and `parameters`. Put the `sql` where the statement is with `edit_file`, and the `parameters` under the file's `parameters:`. The fields stay the governed ones, so their green badges stay. A chart type or the chart's settings change no SQL: keep them in the draft's `charts:` ([Sandbox: charts](vfs://guides/sandbox-charts.md)), and the pencil opens the draft drawn that way.
3. **Show it.** `kater run --select <the draft>` gives you the rows, and `kater brainstorm show <the draft>` draws it for the person. Say what changed in plain words, such as "by quarter" or "every platform", and that the original stays as it is.
4. **Keep it only when they ask,** through the same choices as Save in Kater, each through `kater_apply`, so the person agrees first:
   - **Save as a duplicate**, for everyone, whatever their access to the workspace: `kater scratch duplicate --request-file -` with `{"path": "<the draft>", "workspace_label": "<the copy's label>", "label": "<the chart's label>"}`. Without `workspace_label` the copy is "<the workspace's label> copy", and without `label` the query and the chart have no label and show Kater's words. Send `label` only when the person wrote a title. When the person has a copy of the workspace already, `save.copies` lists them, newest first: save into one with `"into": "<the copy's folder>"` in place of `workspace_label`, unless they ask for a new copy. One commit copies the workspace into `users/<their email>/workspaces/`, with `copied_from` naming the original, writes the draft over the copy's query and chart of the same name, and removes the draft. Into a copy that exists, only the query and the chart are written. It answers the copy's `folder` and `open`, the address of its dashboard. In the copy, every query and tile that reads the query reads the change too: check them, and say which one now shows something its words do not say. Tell the person where their copy is, as a link, and that the original stays as it is.
   - **Save as a new query**, when `save.editable` is true: `kater scratch add --request-file -` with the draft's `path`, `statement: 0`, `destination: {"workspace": "<workspace>"}`, `query: {"label": …}` only when the person wrote a title (without it the query has no label, shows Kater's words and is named from them), `include_chart: true`, and `place`, the place whose chart it came from: `{"kind": "dashboard", "path": "<dashboard.yaml>", "chart": "<the tile's chart>"}`, `{"kind": "app", "path": "<page.html>", "tag": N}`, `{"kind": "project", "path": "<kater.project.yaml>", "metric": "<name>"}`, `{"kind": "for_you"}` or `{"kind": "chat"}`. One commit writes the new query with `copied_from`, the workspace's query order and the place's reference, so the tile, the tag or the metric shows the new query; the original stays as it is, and the draft goes. When the draft's SQL is the query's own, because only its chart changed, the same request writes a new chart of that query and no second query: `query.status` answers `existing`, and `chart` names the new chart. A tile or a tag moves to the new chart in that commit, and a project's metric, a For you card and a chat's answer keep their own chart. Tell the person which one happened. Send `include_chart: true` for any place, so a draft that changed its fields and its chart keeps that chart too.
   - **Replace the original**, when `save.editable` is true: first `kater scratch readers --request-file -` with `{"path": "<the draft>"}`, and tell the person what reads it, by owner, and how many cards of other people. Then `kater scratch replace` with `{"path": "<the draft>", "label": …, "expected": <readers' revision>}`, and `place`, as for Save as a new query, when the draft came from a tile or a tag. Everything that reads the query reads the new version. The same commit writes the chart the draft's `charts:` keeps: over the chart `place` shows, else the query's chart named as the query, else its first chart, else as a new chart of the query. A draft that keeps none leaves the query's charts as they are.
   - **Send to the owner**, when they cannot change the workspace: `kater scratch draft.send --request-file -` with `{"draft": "<the draft>", "note": "<what they want, in their words>"}`. The owner (`save.owner`) gets it in their Kater inbox, and the draft stays.
5. **Leave it.** A draft that is not kept stays in the person's brainstorm folder, and the pencil on the chart opens it again. `kater scratch drafts --request-file -` with the `connection` lists their drafts, and `kater scratch draft.remove` with its `path`, through `kater_apply`, removes one they do not want. A draft made in a local copy stays in that copy, and the pencil in the portal does not open it ([brainstorm](vfs://guides/brainstorm.md)).

A person with the Explorer role may always draft: their brainstorm folder is theirs. Never save to a workspace, a dashboard or an app as a way around a refusal: save it as a duplicate, or send the draft to the owner.
